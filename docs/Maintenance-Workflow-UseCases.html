Fleet Maintenance Workflow

A digital ticket lifecycle that replaces the WhatsApp maintenance relay

📅 28 June 2026 ✅ Live in the system 🔧 Backend + Screens shipped

The Challenge

Why we built this

Today, a car that needs the workshop is handled over WhatsApp messages between the inspector, the drivers, and the workshop controllers. That creates real problems:

  • No record of who asked for what, or when.
  • Cars sit in the workshop and nobody can say at a glance how many or for how long.
  • Costs and downtime are not tied back to the car's history.
  • A car can be sent to the workshop while it is still rented — with no warning.

We replaced that chat thread with a single, enforced digital workflow. Every car moves through clear stages, each handled by the right person, with a permanent record.

The Detail

Use cases
UC-1

Driver requests an inspection

Who: Driver  ·  Goal: Flag a car that seems to have a problem

A driver picks the car and submits a request with a short note (e.g. "pulling to the left when braking"). It lands in the Inspector's queue and Abu Maroof is notified immediately. The car is not yet counted as "in the workshop" — it's only a request.

Business value: every problem report is logged with who raised it and when, instead of a chat message that gets lost.
UC-2

Inspector runs a diagnostic (test drive)

Who: Inspector  ·  Goal: Decide whether the car actually needs the workshop

The inspector test-drives the car and records findings from a ready-made checklist of common issues (engine, brakes, tyres, electrical, A/C, bodywork…) plus any custom note.

Business value: a diagnostic step before a workshop ticket means healthy cars are never sent to the garage unnecessarily — saving downtime and cost.
UC-3

Inspector files the report — needs work, or all clear

Who: Inspector  ·  Goal: Turn a diagnosis into a decision

  • Needs maintenance → a maintenance ticket is created, Logistics is notified to move the car.
  • No maintenance needed → the car is cleared and released. No ticket, no downtime.

Either way the report is saved permanently, and the driver who first requested it is told the result automatically.

Business value: a clear, auditable yes/no decision — and the person who raised the issue always hears back.
UC-4

Logistics dispatches the car to the garage

Who: Logistics / Driver  ·  Goal: Move the car to the workshop, on the record

The driver records the odometer reading, attaches an odometer photo taken on the phone, and selects the garage/vendor. Only now is the car officially "in the workshop", and the controllers are notified. The system also automatically links the car's open maintenance contract.

Business value: mileage and a photo are captured at hand-over, so there's no dispute about the car's condition or reading when it went in.
UC-5

Garage repairs the car and adds findings

Who: Garage (via Logistics)  ·  Goal: Record what was actually done

The car is marked under repair. The garage can add its own findings on top of the inspector's — but it cannot edit or re-report what the inspector already filed. Findings stay in two clearly-labelled buckets:

Inspector findings Garage findings
Business value: a clean, tamper-proof trail of who-found-what — useful for warranty claims and spotting recurring faults.
UC-6

Car is ready; inspector signs off and closes

Who: Inspector  ·  Goal: Confirm the repair and return the car to service

When the garage marks the car ready, it must capture a final odometer reading + photo (the "after" picture matching the "before"). The inspector re-inspects and closes the ticket — which requires the cost and vendor to be recorded. The car is automatically returned to available.

Business value: no car returns to the fleet without a documented cost, a final reading, and a sign-off.
UC-7

Follow-ups while a car is in the workshop

Who: Driver / Logistics  ·  Goal: Keep the conversation on the car, not in WhatsApp

While a car is in transit or under repair, drivers can post follow-up notes (e.g. "garage says part arrives Sunday"). The controllers are notified.

Business value: the status conversation lives on the car's record, visible to everyone, forever.

UC-9

"My Queue" — each person sees only their own work

Who: Inspector / Driver  ·  Goal: Know what I need to do next

  • Inspector sees: pending inspections + cars awaiting final sign-off.
  • Driver sees: active dispatches + cars waiting for a follow-up.
Business value: no one has to scan the whole board to find their tasks.
UC-10

Full history on the car's profile

Who: Management / Anyone  ·  Goal: See everything that ever happened to a car

Each vehicle profile shows a unified timeline: every workflow step (tagged Test-Drive vs Repair), every follow-up note, and a before/after photo gallery of odometer and condition shots, plus a health banner (in workshop / service due / healthy).

Business value: the complete maintenance story of any car, on one screen — for resale, audits, or disputes.
UC-11

Guardrail: no workshop while rented

Who: System (automatic)  ·  Goal: Protect revenue

If a car is rented, opening a maintenance ticket never steals its "rented" status, and any rental↔maintenance overlap is flagged as a violation on the operations dashboard.

Business value: the system actively prevents the costly mistake of pulling a car a customer is still paying for.
